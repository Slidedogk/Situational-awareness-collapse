I — The man

Leopold Aschenbrenner — born roughly 2002, twenty-four in 2026 — was a researcher at OpenAI before publishing the 18-Month AI Intelligence Plan in 2021 (press; AsiaOne). The same press that named him “Nostradamus of AI” later described the fund he built on that thesis: a concentrated, leveraged long book in the physical stack of the AI supercycle — memory chips, power plants, and the cloud providers wiring them together — AsiaOne, ~2026-07-09; press consensus, dossier §5 [press-sourced].

The strategy as the public record legibly states it: borrow against equities, concentrate the longs in a handful of AI/semiconductor/memory names, and trade the options overlay with the market — long calls on Intel and CoreWeave when the tape cooperated (2025-12-31: $746.8M and $774.4M respectively), a put book on the semi leaders and a $2.0B VanEck Semiconductor ETF put when it stopped — SEC EDGAR, 13F-HR infoTables, periods 2025-12-31 / 2026-03-31 [verified].

Full-fund size is one of the few open figures: the press places it at ≈$45B including a private Anthropic stake and leverage, with variants up to $57B by outlet — press, July 2026 [unconfirmed-exact].

Explained — the three words this case turns on

Hedge fund
A private investment pool open only to institutions and wealthy individuals, and — crucially — largely exempt from the disclosure rules that govern mutual funds. Nobody forces a hedge fund to tell you what it owns. The public only sees a hedge fund through the narrow keyholes this page uses: quarterly 13F filings, block-position filings, and whatever the press can confirm.
Leverage
Investing with borrowed money. If you hold $1 and borrow $4, a 10% gain on the position earns 50% on your dollar — and a 10% loss erases half of it. Leverage is why the fund could report a $20 billion book without anything like $20 billion of investor money, and it is the single mechanical reason the July collapse was so fast.
Margin call
The broker's demand to post more collateral when the value of a leveraged position falls. If the investor cannot pay, the broker sells the positions at whatever price the market offers to recover the loan. Forced selling begets further price falls, which trigger more calls — the cascade that emptied this book in days.

II — The run-up

79× in six quarters. Two engines: LP money and markups.

The first reported book, 31 December 2024, is a study in the theme: six names — Marvell $86.8M, Vistra $59.1M, Vertiv $51.7M — the data-center and power pick-and-shovel list — SEC EDGAR, 13F-HR, period 2024-12-31 [verified]. By March 2025 the book had quadrupled to $1.01B across 12 names, and for the first time the leverage is visible: Intel long calls, $459.6M, beside Broadcom $117.2M and Onto Innovation $71.2M — SEC EDGAR, period 2025-03-31 [verified].

June 2025: $2.12B, and the hedge layer is born — a VanEck Semiconductor ETF put at $570.1M — the fund begins trading both sides of the semi complex. September: $4.14B across 28 names, the neocloud sleeve arriving (CoreWeave $563.2M, Core Scientific $362.0M, Intel calls $679.0M). December 2025: $5.52B, 29 names, Bloom Energy the largest at $875.5M — power, compute, and leverage all pointing one direction — SEC EDGAR, periods 2025-06-30 / 09-30 / 12-31 [verified].

Then the two quarters that define the case. March 2026: $13.68B, 42 positions — AUM triples in a single quarter — carrying a $2.04B ETF put and $1.57B NVIDIA put alongside the longs. June 2026: $20.24B in 26 positions, and the concentration reaches its apex: SanDisk $5.674B + Micron $5.574B = $11.25B = 55.6% of the entire public book in one macro-theme — the memory supercycle — SEC EDGAR, periods 2026-03-31 / 06-30 [verified]. Growth in those quarters came from two engines — new LP capital and markups on the concentrated longs — with borrowing against the book in between — press strategy attribution [press-sourced].

III — The week of July

The reversal, the calls,
the sale.

July 2026 was, per JPMorgan cited in the press, the worst month on record for the tech hedge-fund category: −10.2% — JPMorgan via press [press-sourced]. For this fund the category's month was a liquidation event: the memory momentum names broke, margin calls hit the leveraged longs, and deleveraging stopped being a choice — press consensus [press-sourced]. Its July equity loss is reported at ~67% — press headline figure [unconfirmed-exact].

And then the transaction that defines the case. On 30 July 2026, the fund sold the bulk of its public stock portfolio to Citadel — at a discount, per sources — Reuters, Anirban Sen & Manya Saini, New York, 2026-07-30 [press-sourced].

Two details refuse the simple version of the story. First, timing: in the same days — 29 June through 2 July — EDGAR shows the fund still opening and adjusting a 19.9% position in SharonAI (Forms 3/4), a position it amended on 14 August — SEC EDGAR, CUSIP 778920306 [verified]. Second, appetite: days after the rout, the press reports a $400M deployment into the chip startup Source Foundry — press, ~2026-07-10 [press-sourced]. A fund under fire was still a fund taking new size into new name-risk.

Take $100 of position at five-to-one leverage: $80 is borrowed, $20 is the fund's own. Drag the decline and watch the cushion. Around −15%, the broker makes the first call. At −20%, equity is gone — the broker sells, whatever the price. This is why a fund can report a $20 billion book without anything like $20 billion behind it, and why July's reversal became a sale instead of a drawdown. The press figure of −67% equity is, on this arithmetic, roughly a −13% move in the book itself. Leverage converts an ordinary bad month into an extinction event.

IV — The buyer

The cleanest evidence in the case
is the buyer's exit speed.

Citadel took the bulk — not, per the reporting, the entirety — of a public book that on 30 June was worth $20.24B in the fund's own 13F, at what the reporting calls a discount — Reuters, 2026-07-30 [press-sourced]. On 21 August, Ken Griffin told clients that Citadel had since unwound more than 80% of the aggregate risk from the acquired book, inside roughly three weeks — Reuters (Sen, Basil); IBTimes; Crypto Briefing, 2026-08-21/22 [press-sourced].

Read the three facts together — a $20.2B concentrated mark on 30 June; a discounted bulk sale on 30 July; and a $20-scale position's risk discarded 4/5 inside three weeks — and the inference is the finding: this was not a portfolio being inherited. A buyer sophisticated enough to move that size at a price, who then flushed the risk in under a month, was marking the book where the market put it in July, not where the 13F put it in June. The public record states the discount; the buyer's behavior states the rest — inference from [verified] + [press-sourced], dossier §6.

Explained — why memory, and what the hatching means

The memory trade
Every AI server gorges on memory: DRAM for working data and NAND for storage, with HBM (high-bandwidth memory, DRAM stacked in layers beside the GPU) as the scarce ingredient of the 2025–26 buildout. SanDisk (the flash business Western Digital spun off in 2025) and Micron are two of the few companies on earth that make it at scale — so a bet on "the AI buildout keeps compounding" becomes, very quickly, a bet on two memory balance sheets.
Call option
A paid contract that behaves like leveraged ownership: it rises and falls faster than the stock itself. The fund's long calls on Intel and CoreWeave were leveraged upside — cheap to blow up in either direction, which is exactly how the book grew so fast.
Put option (hatched bars)
A paid contract that pays out when a stock falls — insurance, or a stealth short. The diagonal hatching on this page marks every put in the book. By March 2026 the fund held roughly $6.2B of puts on NVIDIA, Oracle, Broadcom, AMD, TSMC and a semiconductor ETF: a $13.7B book that was simultaneously betting on the AI trade (memory longs) and against its biggest winners (the put book). That contradiction is the strategy.

Explained — the form dictionary

13F-HR
The quarterly holdings report every investment manager with over $100M in US-listed equities must file within 45 days of quarter-end. It shows what was held at the quarter's end — never what happened next, and never private holdings. The 45-day lag is why the public did not see the July collapse until mid-August.
13D / 13G
Filed within days of crossing 5% of a single company. A 13D says "active stake, we may influence or push changes"; a 13G says "passive stake, purely investment." Amendments (D/A, G/A) track every move above threshold — which is how this page can watch Core Scientific go 5.8% → 9.4% → 4.4%.
Form D
The notice filed when a fund raises money privately ("Regulation D"). It reveals the vehicle structure — the Partners, Offshore and series entities — but never the dollar amounts. That silence is why the ≈$45B peak figure comes from press reporting, not from any filing.
Forms 3 / 4
Insider transaction records: Form 3 opens an ownership file, Form 4 reports each trade. The fund's SharonAI Forms 3 and 4 (June–July 2026) show it still building a 19.9%-capped position days before the collapse — and amending it on 14 August, after.

What a filing can say.
And what only a newsroom can.

Three confidence levels, used consistently across this page. Verified — read straight from a primary document on SEC EDGAR; the link opens it. Press-sourced — attributed to a named outlet; the page reproduces the reporting, it does not re-verify it. Unconfirmed — the figure varies by outlet or is a single source's estimate; treated as a range, not a fact.

Method: EDGAR company submission feeds (data.sec.gov/submissions) for the 13F/13D/13G/D/F3/F4 sets; full-text search for the phrase across all filers; per-accession primary documents (infoTable XML, cover pages) fetched and summed. This page is a research artifact, not investment advice, and quotes the record rather than the rumor.

Verified

If the 13F says 26, it is 26. Primary document read and, where possible, summed. The number ships with its accession.

Press-sourced

A named newsroom, a date, a byline where reported. We reproduce it as reporting — including its “sources say” — never as fact.

Unconfirmed

The figure moves between outlets, or no primary document exists for it. It appears with ≈, in a range, or tagged — and it never enters the AUM arithmetic.
